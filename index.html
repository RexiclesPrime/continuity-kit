1. Packet Basics

Identity and versioning for the Continuity Packet and Master Index.

What is this and why does it matter?

This is the cover sheet for everything else. It tells your Continuity Agents whose packet this is, which version they are looking at, and when it was last reviewed.

  • Continuity Packet — the single authoritative set of documents your agents open first.
  • Version — a simple number so people know they have the latest copy.
  • Next Review — a reminder date so the packet does not become outdated.

Good enough for a first pass: put your legal name and today’s date.

2. Continuity Team

Designate the people who will act if you are incapacitated or deceased.

What is a Continuity Team?

These are the specific people you authorize to step in and handle practical matters when you cannot.

  • Primary Digital Continuity Agent — unlocks password manager, cloud, domains, and technical systems. Often a technically capable friend rather than a grieving spouse.
  • Family / Estate Liaison — handles family communication and coordinates with the attorney.

Good enough for a first pass: name one trusted technical person and one family member. Talk to them.

3. Legal & Estate Documents

Status and location of core legal instruments.

Why list legal documents here?

Agents need to know where the official papers are. “I think it’s in a drawer” is not sufficient for banks or courts.

  • Will — who gets what and names an executor.
  • Durable Power of Attorney — handles money while you are alive but incapacitated.
  • Healthcare POA / Advance Directive — medical decisions and care wishes.

Good enough: note what exists and where it lives. “Not started” is still useful.

4. Final Wishes

Preferences for disposition, service, and related decisions.

Why record final wishes?

These decisions happen under stress. Clear notes reduce conflict and cost.

Good enough: burial vs. cremation and any strong “please do / please don’t” preferences.

5. Immediate Care

Pets, dependents, and other needs in the first 24–48 hours.

What belongs here?

Who takes the dog? Who picks up the kids? This is operational guidance, not a legal guardianship designation.

Good enough: list pets and one temporary contact if you have dependents. Leave blank if not applicable.

6. Personal Letters / Nothing Left Unsaid

Short notes to specific people. These are often the most valued part of a packet.

Why write these?

Most people never get around to saying the important things. A few sentences left for a spouse, child, or friend can matter more than any account password.

  • You do not need polished prose.
  • You can write one letter or several.
  • Optional “when to deliver” note (immediately, after the service, privately, etc.).

Good enough: one short note to the most important person. You can always add more later.

Good enough: list 3–5 items that would upset people if they disappeared.

9. Digital Disposition Preferences

What should happen to accounts after access is obtained.

Beyond just getting in

Access is only half the job. Tell your agents whether to memorialize, download and archive, transfer, or delete.

  • Social media (memorialize vs. delete)
  • Email (preserve for a period, then close)
  • Photos and files (download for family)
  • Domains and websites (transfer or let expire)

Good enough: a few sentences on the accounts that matter most to you or your family.

Why this matters

Once the password manager is open, agents still need to get into your phone or computer, and they may need backup codes if 2FA is enabled.

Good enough: phone PIN and where authenticator backup codes are stored.

11. Break-Glass Access

How Continuity Agents will unlock the credential database and encrypted USB.

What is Break-Glass?

The controlled emergency method for opening the password database and any encrypted USB that holds the packet. It must be usable under stress and still protected from casual discovery.

  • Sealed envelope — contains the master password (and optionally a YubiKey). Agents know where it is; others do not.
  • YubiKey — can act as a second factor (or primary factor) for the KeePassXC database via Challenge-Response. Even with the password, the physical key is still required.
  • Never put the live master password in the Continuity Packet itself. The packet only says where the sealed envelope and USB are stored.

Good enough for a first pass: decide where the sealed envelope and encrypted USB will live and write those locations here.

How to encrypt a USB drive (Mac, Windows, Linux)

macOS

  • Open Disk Utility → select the USB → Erase.
  • Format: APFS (Encrypted) or Mac OS Extended (Journaled, Encrypted).
  • Set a strong password. Store that password in the sealed envelope (not in this packet).
  • Alternatively use VeraCrypt for cross-platform compatibility.

Windows

  • Right-click the USB drive → Turn on BitLocker.
  • Choose “Use a password to unlock the drive.”
  • Save the recovery key in the sealed envelope (print it; do not rely only on a Microsoft account).
  • VeraCrypt is also an option if you need Mac/Linux compatibility.

Linux

  • Use cryptsetup (LUKS): sudo cryptsetup luksFormat /dev/sdX then sudo cryptsetup open /dev/sdX continuity.
  • Or use VeraCrypt for a GUI and cross-platform volume.
  • Store the LUKS passphrase in the sealed envelope.

Place the KeePassXC .kdbx file and the generated packet PDFs/Markdown on the encrypted USB. Keep a second encrypted copy in a different physical location.

Using a YubiKey with KeePassXC

KeePassXC supports YubiKey via Challenge-Response (HMAC-SHA1). This can be used in addition to a master password or, with care, as a stronger factor.

  • In KeePassXC: Database → Database Security → Add additional protection → Add YubiKey Challenge-Response.
  • Program the YubiKey slot (usually slot 2) with a Challenge-Response secret. KeePassXC can do this, or use the YubiKey Manager.
  • Store the YubiKey itself with the sealed envelope (or in a known location the agents can reach). A second YubiKey programmed with the same secret is recommended as a backup.
  • Test the unlock with both agents before you consider the setup complete.
  • The sealed envelope should still contain the master password so the database can be opened if the YubiKey is lost or damaged.

Official KeePassXC documentation covers the exact clicks; the critical point for continuity is that agents know where the YubiKey is and that a password recovery path exists.

16. Storage & Review

Where copies of the packet live and how often it is reviewed.

Where should the packet live?

Primary physical + sealed envelope, a geographic backup, and an encrypted digital copy. Review at least annually.

17. What Next?

Steps to take once the packet is filled and generated.

The packet is only useful if people know it exists and can act on it.

Filling out this form is the foundation. The next actions turn it into a working continuity plan.

  • Talk to your Continuity Agents — walk them through the packet, confirm they accept the role, and show them where the physical copies and sealed envelope will live.
  • Talk to your estate attorney — review the Legal section, confirm documents are current, and ask them to add or update a digital-asset clause if needed.
  • Set a review schedule — put a recurring reminder (calendar, phone, or annual tradition) so the packet does not go stale.
  • Store the copies — print the PDFs, place the break-glass materials in a sealed envelope, and put everything where you said it would be.
  • Test once — have one agent locate the packet and open the KeePassXC database without your help. Fix any friction.

Good enough for a first pass: schedule the two conversations and put the review date on your calendar before you close this page.
